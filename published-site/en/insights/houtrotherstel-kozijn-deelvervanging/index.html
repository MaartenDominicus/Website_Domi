At a glance

A decayed sill or frame member? See how local repair, timber splicing, glazing and painting fit together.

01

Look beneath the paint

A small blister in paint may conceal deeper damage. The project photograph shows removed frame timber with large cavities. Have the adjacent joints and source of moisture assessed too. Painting over weakened timber will not restore it.

02

Choose repair or partial replacement

A limited defect may suit an appropriate repair system. A more extensively damaged section of sill or frame may need new timber while sound parts remain. Extensive decay can make replacing the complete frame more sensible.

03

Fit the replacement timber

Partial replacement starts by removing decayed timber. The new section must suit the existing profile, joint and structural role. Preparation and installation follow the selected repair system. A loose block fixed into a rotten area is not a complete repair.

04

Include glazing and finishes

Check glazing beads, seals and drainage at the same time. A repair beside an open joint remains vulnerable to moisture. Coordinate glazing and painting before closing the work. The photograph below shows a complete replacement window, which is a different option from splicing timber.

05

Agree the complete scope

Send an overall photograph and a close-up for an initial discussion. Mention sticking windows and soft timber. Agree whether removing glass, temporarily closing the opening, new timber, glazing and painting are included. The final method depends on an inspection of the actual frame.
