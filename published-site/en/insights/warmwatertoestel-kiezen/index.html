Which hot-water system suits your home?

A storage cylinder, instantaneous heater and heat-pump water heater each balance comfort, space, energy and installation differently.

At a glance

A storage cylinder, instantaneous heater and heat-pump water heater each balance comfort, space, energy and installation differently.

01

Map your peak demand

Start with your hot-water profile rather than a brand or power rating. How many people live in the home, how many showers follow one another, and is hot water needed in the kitchen and bathroom at the same time? Peak demand often matters more than average daily use.

02

Compare the three main types

A storage system can deliver a substantial volume for a limited period, but needs space and loses some heat while idle. An instantaneous unit heats on demand and avoids a large cylinder, but may require considerable electrical or gas capacity. A heat-pump water heater uses ambient heat and needs suitable space, airflow and provision for noise and condensate drainage.

03

Check technical constraints

Check what the property can support. The electrical supply, distribution board, flue or ventilation arrangement, pipe diameters and distance to outlets may restrict the options. A unit close to the main outlets reduces waiting time and distribution losses, but it must remain safely accessible for service.

04

Calculate total cost

Compare more than purchase price. Include installation alterations, expected energy use, servicing, useful life and any space sacrificed. An efficient product that does not match the household pattern can still be uncomfortable or unnecessarily costly.

05

Plan for the home and its future

Base the final choice on a survey of the property and an estimate of hot-water demand. Ask for a proposal that clearly addresses capacity, reheating time, available infrastructure and future plans. The result should still make sense after a renovation or change in household size.
