At a glance

Flickering lights, warm outlets and repeated trips are not minor details. They can indicate load, connection or protection problems.

01

Flickering or dimming lights

Lights that repeatedly flicker or dim may point to a loose connection, faulty fitting or voltage change when a heavy appliance starts. Note when it happens and which equipment is running. That pattern helps distinguish a single lighting fault from a wider installation issue.

02

Heat, discolouration or a scorched smell

A plug, outlet or switch should not become noticeably hot, discoloured or smell scorched. Stop using it when any of these signs appears. Remove a plug only if it is safe to do so and have the point checked before it is loaded again.

03

Protection that trips repeatedly

A circuit breaker or residual-current device trips for a reason. A one-off overload differs from repeated failure involving the same appliance or circuit. Do not keep resetting protection without understanding the cause; that does not remove the fault.

04

Loose points and too many power strips

Loose outlets, damaged flexes and chains of power strips increase the risk of poor connections and overload. Temporary extension leads are not a substitute for enough fixed outlets. When a room layout or equipment changes, a planned extension is generally neater and safer.

05

Assess the system as a whole

Arrange an assessment when several signs occur together, when buying an older property or before a major renovation. A competent inspection considers circuit distribution, protective devices, earthing, connections and intended load, addressing the system rather than only the visible symptom.
