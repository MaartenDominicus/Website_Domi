Choosing a bathroom extractor: capacity, noise and control

At a glance

Good ventilation removes moisture without sacrificing comfort or quiet. These choices determine whether the system works in practice.

01

Remove the full moisture peak

A bathroom produces a large amount of moisture in a short time. Without sufficient extraction, it remains on mirrors, sealant, walls and ceilings. The fan must suit the room and run long enough to remove the moisture peak after a shower.

02

Calculate more than room volume

Required performance depends on room volume, duct length and resistance, and the available supply of dry air. A powerful fan performs poorly when the door seals completely or the duct has many bends and restrictions. Supply and extract need to be designed as one airflow path.

03

Control noise at its source

Consider noise in the real installation. A quiet motor helps, but vibration, a narrow duct or poorly mounted backdraught shutter can still be audible. Position, duct material and mounting matter as much as the number on the packaging.

04

Choose a suitable control method

An overrun timer keeps extraction running after use. A humidity sensor responds to rising moisture and helps when users forget to switch ventilation on. Linking the fan to the light is simple but does not always provide the right run time. Match the control method to how the room is used.

05

Complete the extract route

Check where the air ultimately goes. Discharging into a closed loft or crawl space simply moves the moisture problem. Use a suitable route outdoors or connect correctly to the existing ventilation system. Bathroom electrical connections also need equipment and installation suited to the relevant safety zones.
