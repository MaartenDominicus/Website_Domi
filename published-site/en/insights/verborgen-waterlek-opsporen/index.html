At a glance

A higher water bill, musty smell or cool damp patch may be the first warning. Here is how to assess the situation safely and methodically.

01

Recognise the early signs

A hidden leak rarely reveals itself immediately. Watch for unexplained water use, discoloured paint or plaster, lifting skirting boards, mould and an area that remains unusually cool or damp. One sign proves little, but several signs in the same area deserve prompt attention.

02

Use the water meter as a first check

Make sure no water is intentionally being used, then record the water-meter reading. If it changes while taps, appliances and toilets are inactive, there may be unwanted flow. Only isolate appliances or branch valves when you know how to do so safely; the result can help a technician narrow down the affected part of the system.

03

Do not search only at the damp patch

The visible damp patch may be some distance from the source. Water can travel along a pipe, joist or floor edge before appearing. Avoid opening a wall or ceiling at random. Moisture measurements, pressure checks and knowledge of pipe routes can often reduce the search area first.

04

Limit further damage

Limit further damage while you wait. For an active leak, close the main water valve, isolate electrical circuits in the wet area if this can be done safely, and move vulnerable belongings. Photograph the situation and note when it became visible; this helps with repair planning and any insurance report.

05

Repair the cause as well

Repairing the pipe is not always the end of the job. Affected materials need controlled drying before finishes can be restored. Ask for the cause to be assessed too—such as a poor joint, wear, movement or frost—so the same failure is less likely to recur elsewhere.
