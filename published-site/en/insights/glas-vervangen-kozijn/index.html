At a glance

A new pane needs more than a glass measurement. Our project photographs illustrate the frame, glazing beads and surrounding finishes.

01

Start with your needs

Are you replacing damaged glass, addressing draughts or changing how a window opens? Explain the aim first. Overall exterior and interior photographs help the discussion. The photograph above shows fitted glazed windows during ongoing renovation.

02

Assess the existing frame

New glass needs a suitable frame. Check the timber, available space and joints. Decay may require a repair first, or frame replacement may be more appropriate. Measuring only the visible glass is not enough to order a replacement.

03

Match the glass and glazing beads

The glass build-up and weight, rebate and glazing beads must work together. Beads retain the pane and, together with seals, complete its junction with the frame. Discuss safety, ventilation and appearance before confirming the specification.

04

Complete the junction

Fitting a window does not complete every surrounding task. The detail photograph shows the glazed window but unfinished wallwork above it. Glazing, seals and junctions with timber or masonry belong in a coordinated sequence.

05

Agree the finishing work

Clarify whether removing old glass, waste disposal, new beads and finishing are included. Agree who handles painting or plastering. Keep drainage and junctions clear during routine maintenance. Each party should know which part of the work they are delivering.
